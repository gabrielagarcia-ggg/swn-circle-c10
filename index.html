Session Resource · TED Talk

Watch before Nov 25
🎤
TEDxWilmingtonSalon · 2022
"The New Way to Brag in the Modern World — and Feel Good Doing It"
Henna Pryor · Performance Coach
Watch on TED.com →
Why this talk for our Circle

Every single one of us in this Circle mentioned self-advocacy, visibility, or being seen as a challenge. Henna Pryor flips the script: she shows — backed by research — that sharing your wins isn't arrogance, it's a career skill. And it can be learned.

Her concept of "Good Bragging" is the bridge between staying invisible (which none of us wants) and coming off as obnoxious (which we're all afraid of). The sweet spot is being seen as both competent and likable — and she teaches exactly how.

Key Ideas from the Talk
Sharing wins = career survival
In the hybrid world, if people can't see your work, it doesn't exist. Visibility is no longer optional — it's how you stay relevant and get considered for opportunities.
"Good Bragging" is research-backed
It's not just a feel-good concept. Studies show that the most successful professionals are perceived as both highly competent AND highly likable — not one or the other.
Competence + Likability = the formula
Most people sacrifice one for the other. Good Bragging is the method that lets you claim both — by being specific, warm, and connecting your win to impact on others.
It's a learnable skill
Self-promotion isn't a personality trait. It's a skill with a framework. If Stephany makes everyone else shine and disappears, or Kerry holds back cross-team — this is for them.
Homework — Watch before Session 2 · Nov 25
"Think of a win from the last 6 months that you never talked about out loud. Come ready to share it using her framework — specific, impact-connected, warm, and unapologetic."

POSITION — How do I communicate my value?

Four members came to this session carrying wins they had never said out loud. Through the Impact Story formula — what happened, what I did, what changed, and why it mattered — each one found words for work that had been invisible: a $14M telecom deal orchestrated quietly for a year, executive messaging that stopped an internal war, a Severity 1 crisis that rebuilt a customer's trust, and a year of showing up new that became a story of real growth. The session's central realization: self-promotion is not arrogance — it's making sure the people who matter know what you're actually doing.

Impact Stories — Each Member's Win

GG
Gabriela Garcia $14M TCB Telecom Deal
What I did
For one full year, articulated the installed base of a major telecom, identified a new Data Cloud opportunity, brought in the right specialists, and kept the client engaged through difficult moments — including being kicked out of the office.
Impact & realization
Closed $14M TCB (+$3M new Data Cloud ACB). A co-worker told her: "Gabby, if you weren't there, we wouldn't have gotten the money." She wasn't the main closer — but she was the orchestrator who made it possible.
SF
Stephany Fletcher Executive Messaging on Complex Program
What I did
Translated highly technical, complex program details into executive messaging so leaders on both sides of an internal conflict could understand and make decisions.
Impact
Became the confidant both sides of leadership trusted. Reduced infighting by creating shared understanding. The leaders on each team came to her for perspective on the other — invisible glue work made visible.
CL
Christina Lavash Severity 1 — Revenue Cloud Crisis
What I did
Coordinated internal product and support teams with a customer completely blocked from making quotes. Got both sides talking, ran daily standups, pushed for full context even when both sides were defensive.
Impact
Identified a Salesforce-side bug, unblocked the customer's daily operations, and restored their confidence in the platform. Also unlocked the account team's ability to have strategic conversations again. Seen by product, sales, and customer leadership.
KH
Kerry Hughes Dreamforce Budget & Year of Growth
What I did
Learned a complex budgeting system from scratch, managed Dreamforce budget across branding, finance, design, and external agencies, and kept all moving pieces on track under a hard deadline. Also went from never having heard of a program to running full leadership meetings on it.
Impact
Single view of budget → cleaner reporting → successful Dreamforce. No longer "net new" — fully part of the team. The year of onboarding IS the impact story: she showed up, learned fast, and delivered.

Key Discussion Moments

Self-promotion ≠ self-congratulation
Gabriela named it out loud: "In my mind, saying 'I did it' feels arrogant. But there are people who do 10% of what I do and they own the room." The circle recognized this as a shared pattern.
Invisible work is still work
Kerry: "Wins are not always as clear-cut as closing a huge deal. Sometimes it's those smaller in-between moments — the background things that keep the visible things working well."
The "commanding women" double standard
Kerry: "We're conditioned to be the nice one. If a guy did the same thing it wouldn't feel out of the ordinary." Gabriela: being direct reads differently for women — but results are results.
STAR + Value = the full formula
Christina: the impact formula mirrors STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) but adds the key layer most people skip — why did it actually matter? That's what turns a task into an impact story.
